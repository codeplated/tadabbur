# Tadabbur — Quartz Site

> Bismillah. This repo publishes the Tadabbur knowledge base as a website.

---

## What This Repo Does

Takes the markdown vault generated by the Tadabbur generator repo and
builds a fully navigable website using Quartz 5. It is deployed to
Cloudflare Pages at `tadabbur.belambo.com` — see Deployment below.

## PROTECTED — Do Not Touch Without Strong Reason

### `/content` folder — GENERATED CONTENT

Every file inside `/content` is generated by the Tadabbur generator repo
and synced here. **Do not manually edit any file inside `/content`.**

- Edits will be overwritten the next time the generator runs
- If content is wrong, fix it in the generator repo, not here
- Don't work around generator output with Quartz-side remaps either —
  change the generator
- The front page (slug `index`) hides its title, date, breadcrumbs and
  properties box via the `condition: not-index` layout condition in
  `quartz.config.yaml`. The graph is off it by the same condition: there it
  drew only the page's own links, in a 256 by 286px box beside the hero.

The site has four sections, and they appear the same way in two places: as
four cards on the front page and as the four parents in the left panel. In
order: **The Quran, Themes, Asma Ul Husna, Personalities** (owner's order,
2026-10-17; the generator's front page sets it, and the menu follows). Each is a
top-level folder in `content/`, so the generator decides the structure and the
site only orders and styles it. Themes is the folder `tags`, because `/tags`
is where the site serves a theme's page; its index note is titled "Themes".

The four pages that list a section (The Quran, Themes, Personalities, Asma Ul
Husna) are built one way. Each is an index, not a table: every entry under a
heading, in columns, with a filter box, a row of arrangements and a switch for
details.

| Page          | Grouped by | Other arrangements             | Details (the switch)                  |
| ------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| The Quran     | in order   | Meccan / Medinan, A–Z, Longest | English name, revelation, Urdu; on    |
| Themes        | category   | A–Z, Most Ayaat                | description; off to begin with        |
| Personalities | type       | By Path, A–Z, Most Ayaat       | path, era, Urdu name; off             |
| Asma Ul Husna | category   | 1–99, A–Z, Most in Quran       | meaning in English and Urdu, root; on |

The generator writes each page as plain headings and lists, one line per entry,
which is the whole page with scripts off and in Obsidian. This repo lays those
lines out ("Listing pages" in `custom.scss`) and adds the filter, the
arrangements and the closing groups with one script of its own,
`quartz/components/scripts/indexPage.inline.ts`, which holds what differs
between the pages (labels, arrangements) in one table at its top. A to Z
files a name without its Arabic article, so Al-Hayy is under H: by first
letter, 97 of the 99 Names were under A. On The Quran's page the filter also
takes an ayah reference (2:255, Baqarah 255): it offers "Go to Al-Baqarah
2:255", and Enter follows it.

The Quran's page was 114 picture cards (a `base` block) until 2026-10-16:
13.8 screens on a desktop with nothing to filter by. As a listing it is 3.

Themes was a table until 2026-10-11 (139 rows, 10 to 16 screens). Personalities
was a table with tabs and Asma Ul Husna ten tables until 2026-10-12: 7 to 8
screens on a desktop and 8 to 13 on a phone, where one cut its last column off
and the other broke a three-letter root over two lines; sorting was mouse-only,
and neither could be searched. As indexes all three are 2 to 4 screens on a
desktop and under 2 on a phone, where the groups start closed.

**A surah's page uses the right-hand column** on a desktop, as the listings
do: `surahPage.inline.ts` gathers the picture, the facts and the full list of
themes into an `<aside>` beside the text (below a desktop's width it is
`display: contents` and its parts stay in the flow). On a desktop the panel
stays in view and holds the controls (owner's choice, 2026-10-16, over a panel
that scrolled away and left the column empty while the surah was read). In the
panel the full list of themes is open and is the only part that scrolls, in a
lane of its own; the head's line of the eight most frequent themes is hidden
there, since they head the same list. Below a desktop's width the list is
folded and the line shows.

Each ayah on a surah's page is a way to its own page: a click anywhere on it
that is not a link or a text selection opens it, and its number carries a
chevron. The controls are one row at every width: where the four switches do
not fit (a container query on the row, 40rem) they fold behind a "Show"
button.

**A surah's page is the surah.** Until 2026-10-16 it was a heading, a 640px
picture, a table of themes and a run of ayah numbers: the numbers began 2.3
phone screens down and none of the surah's words were on the page. Now the
generator writes a short head (the surah's name, its English and Urdu names,
where it sits, its eight most frequent themes, each linked to this surah's
ayaat on the theme's page, and every theme folded under them) and then every
ayah in order: its number, which links to the ayah's page, then the Arabic,
English and Urdu and the ayah's themes. `surahPage.inline.ts` adds a bar that
stays at the top while the surah is read: a field that goes to any ayah (255
on this surah, 3:18 or Yasin 12 anywhere) and the number of the ayah being
read, which is also the way back to the top. Switches above the text show or
hide Arabic, English, Urdu and themes, kept in the browser for every surah. An
ayah's text carries its number as an id, so `/the-quran/2---the-cow/#255`
lands on it.

**An ayah's page is the verse first.** The generator writes the head
(`Al-Baqarah 2:255`; the surah's name, which links back to this ayah in the
surah's text; where it sits), the steps to the ayaat either side with the
player between them, the Arabic, English and Urdu, its themes by name, and the
two tafsirs folded in `<details>`. `ayahPage.inline.ts` opens on the next ayah
the tafsirs the reader left open. Until 2026-10-16 it opened with a box of raw
fields and the recitation; on a phone the English was 2,052px down and the
page 8.5 screens. Now it is 932px and 3.6 screens.

A reference typed into search (2:255) goes straight to the ayah on Enter;
other text searches as before. All three places resolve a reference with
`quartz/components/scripts/ayahRef.ts`, from the `static/menu.json` the menu
already fetches, so the file is fetched once.

A person's page and a Name's page draw their own head, as a theme's page does.
The generator writes it into the note: the heading, a line or two under it (a
person's name in Urdu; a Name's meaning in English, then Urdu), and a quote
block that says the fields in lines, with the type or category and every theme
as a link. The note is marked `cssclasses: [profile]`, and the layout conditions
keep the stock title, the properties box, the reading time and the tag chips
off it. Those four put the first section 840 to 1,050px down a desktop screen,
gave the page two `h1`s, and showed fields under their raw names
(`root_meaning`) and themes as keys (`#story_adam`).

**A person's and a Name's ayaat look as a theme's do** (since 2026-10-17).
They come last in the note, under `## Mentioned in Quran` or `## Key Ayaat`: a
heading per surah, then each ayah's reference, its English and its themes.
Until then they were bare links shown as "2_51", which a reader had to open to
learn what they said. `profilePage.inline.ts` gives the page what a theme's
page has around its ayaat: on a desktop a column that stays in view, with the
page's sections and then the surahs and their counts, the one being read
marked (it takes the place of the contents, graph and backlinks there);
anywhere narrower a select of the surahs stuck under the top of the screen
while the ayaat are read. A click anywhere on an ayah opens it. The marking is
`surahIndex.ts`, shared with a theme's page. Search leaves those ayaat out, as
it does a surah's text.

### `node_modules/` — dependencies

Never edit, never commit. Managed by npm.

**Never hand-edit files inside `node_modules/` to fix a plugin bug**, even
temporarily "just to test something." It's gitignored, so the fix vanishes
on the next `npm install` and silently regresses on deploy with no diff to
explain why. If a community plugin needs a behavior change:

1. Edit the file under `node_modules/` to find the right fix.
2. Run `npx patch-package <package-name>` — this diffs your edit against a
   clean copy and writes a `.patch` file into `patches/`.
3. **Pin that package to an exact version in `package.json`** (`"0.1.0"`,
   not `"^0.1.0"`). A patch only applies to the version it was made from.
4. Commit the `patches/` file and `package.json` / `package-lock.json`.

`npm install` / `npm ci` re-apply every patch via the `postinstall` script
(`patch-package`) and fail loudly if one no longer applies. Verify with
`npm ci` and look for a tick next to each patch.

Community plugins ship their components twice: `dist/index.js` and
`dist/components/index.js`. The build loads layout components from
`dist/components/index.js`, so a patch must change **both** files or the
change silently doesn't show up.

To upgrade a patched plugin: bump the exact version, run `npm install`,
fix whatever part of the patch fails, and regenerate it with patch-package.

Why patches and not forks: a local or git-sourced plugin gets symlinked or
cloned into `.quartz/plugins` and built with its own toolchain on every
deploy. Patches keep the stock npm package and only store the diff.

Existing patches:

- `@quartz-community+graph+0.1.0.patch` — three fixes in the graph's inline
  script: (1) decodes `window.location.pathname` so the current page is
  found when the slug contains Arabic (otherwise the local graph can't
  highlight the page you're on); (2) skips `.base` files when building the
  node set, so `Quran.base` isn't a graph hub. It matches on the `.base`
  extension, so it covers any base file the generator emits; (3) returns
  before loading d3 and pixi.js when `(hover: none)` matches, so touch
  devices don't pull ~700KB off a CDN and force-simulate 6,500 nodes for a
  box no finger can usefully pan. `custom.scss` hides the empty frame under
  the same query, so the two always agree — including when a tablet is
  rotated.
- `@quartz-community+folder-page+0.1.0.patch` — the plugin always appends a
  raw file list under a folder's own page. Every surah folder now has an
  `index.md` that lists its ayaat in order, so the appended list repeated all
  286 of them sorted as text (2_1, 2_10, 2_100 …) and undid the point of the
  index. The patch skips the list when the folder note has content of its own;
  a folder without an index still gets the stock listing.
- `@quartz-community+tag-page+0.1.0.patch` — replaces the stock date-sorted
  title list on tag pages with a theme's page. From the top:
  - **A head.** The page's own `h1`, with the theme's Urdu name at the other
    end of the line; its description (the body of `content/tags/<tag>.md`);
    and one line: the category as a link to it on the Themes page, how many
    ayaat in how many surahs, and a link to the related pages. This replaced
    the stock title, a properties box and "1 min read", which the layout
    conditions now keep off these pages.
  - **The surah index, twice.** `nav.tt-jump`, a list of surahs with counts,
    and `div.tt-bar`, a `select` of the same surahs with a "top" link.
    `custom.scss` shows the list in the right-hand column on a desktop and the
    select, stuck under the top of the screen, anywhere narrower.
    `themePage.inline.ts` marks the surah being read in both. They replaced a
    row of up to 77 chips that put the first ayah one to two screens down.
  - **The ayaat**, grouped by surah in Quran order (from `surah`, `ayah`,
    `surah_name`), each as `surah:ayah` with its English translation and its
    other tags. The translation is the first line after the heading containing
    the word `English`, or frontmatter `description` if the generator sets
    one. A surah's Arabic name is marked `lang="ar"`.
  - **Related pages** (personalities, names), after the ayaat, at `#related`.

  It says "ayaat", and "ayah" for one. `/tags` itself is
  `content/tags/index.md`, the Themes page: a folder note, so the folder-page
  plugin renders it and this plugin's own A–Z grid of tags is only what `/tags`
  would fall back to without that note.

- `@quartz-community+footer+0.1.1.patch` — the "Created with Quartz vX" line
  is hardcoded in the plugin, so the patch replaces it with "Created with a heart
  by Belambo" (links to https://belambo.com). The `footer` option links sit
  on the same row, open in a new tab, and a link named `GitHub` renders as
  the GitHub icon instead of text.

Only plugins the site actually uses are in `quartz.config.yaml` and

## Deployment — Cloudflare Pages

The build is verified to work from a clean `npm ci` without `.quartz/` or
`public/`, which is what Cloudflare has.

```
Repository:         github.com/codeplated/tadabbur, branch main
Build command:      npx quartz build -d content
Build output dir:   public
Node version:       22   (from .node-version)
Custom domain:      tadabbur.belambo.com
```

Every push to `main` triggers a build (500 a month on the free plan), so sync
generator output in one commit rather than many.

**No file may be over 25 MiB**: Pages refuses the whole deploy otherwise (it
did on 2026-10-17, when `static/contentIndex.json` reached 33.4 MiB). After a
change to what the generator writes, build and check:
`find public -type f -size +20M`. The search index is the file at risk; what
goes into it is decided in `quartz/plugins/transformers/searchText.ts`.

**DNS is at IONOS, not Cloudflare** (`belambo.com`'s nameservers are
`ui-dns.*`). The custom domain is added in the Pages dashboard, and IONOS holds
a `CNAME tadabbur → <project>.pages.dev` record. The same reason rules out
Worker routes on the domain and Cloudflare Email Routing.

Analytics is Cloudflare Web Analytics, enabled in the Pages dashboard — no
code, which is why `analytics: null` stays.

### Feedback Worker

`feedback-worker/` is deployed separately with wrangler, at
`https://tadabbur-feedback.tadabbur-feedback.workers.dev` (Worker name, then the
account's workers.dev subdomain). It forwards each message to Telegram; the bot
token and chat ID are wrangler secrets and must never be committed — the repo
is public. Setup and checks are in `feedback-worker/README.md`.

Everything runs on free plans. Keep it that way: don't upgrade Workers to
Paid, and don't add D1, KV, R2 or other bindings to the Worker without
discussion.

## The Site Menu

The left sidebar holds the site's own menu, not a file tree. The explorer
plugin was removed on 2026-10-12: it drew all 6,673 notes as rows on every
page (98% of the page's DOM) from the 20 MB content index, was 24 phone
screens long with The Quran open, and put 400 Tab stops before the page.

What the menu is:

- **The sections**, in the order the front page links to them. One is open at a
  time; the others stay in reach under it, because the open one takes the
  height that is left and scrolls by itself.
- **A section's list**: surahs, people, names or themes. A title's leading
  number and trailing Arabic name are shown in their own columns.
- **A surah's ayaat as a grid of numbers**, shown for the surah being read, with
  its name pinned over them. One Tab stop; the arrow keys move through it.
- **The current page** marked with `aria-current`, and the list scrolled to it
  on every screen size.
- **On a phone**, a sheet over the thumb bar (see "Every Screen Counts").
  Closed it is `inert`. Escape closes it and returns focus to the menu button;
  following a link or tapping the page above it closes it.
- **Folding a surah's numbers.** On the surah's own page its name in the menu
  folds the grid of ayah numbers away and brings it back (`aria-expanded`); on
  an ayah's page it is a link to the surah, as before.

Where it comes from: `quartz/util/menu.ts` derives everything from the content
(a section is a top-level folder with an `index.md`), so the generator still
decides the structure and this repo names no folder. The data ships as
`static/menu.json`. Menu names lose their harakat (and ٱ → ا); page content
keeps them.

Column width on a desktop: `clamp(320px, 24vw, 23rem)`. At 23rem all but 3 of
the 114 surah names fit beside their Arabic names; at 320px 16 are cut with an
ellipsis. A name never wraps. On a screen under 520px tall (a phone held
sideways) the whole menu scrolls as one, since four pinned section names would
leave the list no room.

Still to do: `contentIndex.json` (20 MB, 2.7 MB compressed) is no longer needed
by the menu but is still fetched on every page for search and the graph.

## Every Screen Counts the Same

The site has to be equally good on a phone, a tablet, a foldable, a laptop, a
desktop monitor and a high-resolution display. No device is the one that "has
to be right" with the others as fallbacks: a fault at 1512px is as much a
fault as one at 390px. (An earlier version of these notes put phones first, and
desktop faults slipped through because of it.) `PRODUCT.md` records this, along
with who the site is for and its accessibility requirement.

The small-screen rules live in the "Phones and tablets" section at the end of
`quartz/styles/custom.scss`, and the rest of this section explains them.

Two kinds of media query are used there and they are **not** interchangeable:

| Query                     | Means                                | Used for                                                       |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `($mobile)` / `($tablet)` | how much room there is               | layout: the app bar, grid columns, card columns, drawer        |
| `(hover: none)`           | the reader is pointing with a finger | tap-target sizes, and anything that used to depend on hovering |

A touch tablet in landscape is wide enough for the desktop layout but still
needs finger-sized controls, which is why the two are kept apart. `--tap-target`
(2.75rem) is the minimum for anything a finger has to hit; `--thumb-bar`
(3.75rem) is the phone's bar along the foot of the screen, and the menu's
sheet, the feedback form, the page's bottom padding and `scroll-padding-bottom`
measure from it. `--mobile-bar-height` (3.5rem) is the top row.

What the section does, and why, so it doesn't get undone by accident:

- **The thumb bar.** On a phone the controls sit along the foot of the screen,
  where the thumb is, and stay there: from the right, Menu, Search, Feedback,
  Theme, each an icon over its word (owner's choice, 2026-10-16). They are the same elements
  as on a wider screen (`SiteMenuToggle`, the search plugin, Feedback.tsx's
  button, the theme switch), each `position: fixed` to a quarter of the bar;
  the bar is the left sidebar's `::after`. The sidebar's `z-index: 50` makes it
  a layer of its own, so the feedback button, outside it, is at 60. Until then
  they were a row at the top left, the farthest corner from the thumb, and
  scrolled away: base.scss leaves the phone's sidebar unpositioned.
- **The top row** keeps the logo beside the name (one 56px row; stacked, it
  was 179px) and scrolls away with the page. What sticks while reading (a
  surah's controls, a theme page's surah select) sticks to the very top.
- **The menu is a sheet** that rises from behind the thumb bar to 80% of the
  screen, over a dimmed page; a tap on the page above it, Escape or a link
  puts it away. It locks the page behind it. Rows and ayah numbers are
  `--tap-target` tall on touch, in the sheet and the tablet sidebar alike.
- **The phone's one column is exactly the screen's width.** Stock Quartz sizes
  it `auto`, so the bar's site name, which does not wrap, pushed the page
  wider than a 320px screen and pushed the search and theme buttons off-screen
  when the browser's text size was turned up.
- **The graph is skipped on touch** (see Graph View).
- **The right rail stacks** on a phone instead of splitting a 390px screen
  between two widgets, and the backlink list no longer scrolls inside the
  scrolling page.
- **Full-screen UI uses `dvh`, never `vh`** — `100vh` counts browser chrome
  that scrolls away.
- **Search opens at the top of the screen** on mobile; at `12vh` the field and
  most of its results sat behind the keyboard.
- **A surah's go-to bar keeps one row** at every width. Under 440px it says
  "Go to" and "255" rather than "Go to ayah" and "255 of 286" (the words stay
  for a screen reader), and on a closed foldable the four switches tighten to
  stay on one line.
- **The front page's four cards** come before its prose, sit two by two, and
  stack wherever the page's own column cannot hold two cards of 17rem: a phone,
  and a tablet held upright, where that column is 511px beside the menu. The
  whole card is the link to its section. Each card ends with three entries
  from the section; a row there is a link over its whole width, so a name and
  the count beside it go to the same page. With the prose first, the first
  card began 851px down a phone's 844px screen, and none of the four was on the
  first screen of a 1280px laptop.
- **A listing page closes its groups** wherever it has room for one column
  only, so a phone or an upright tablet shows 13 headings on Themes, not 140
  rows (6 on Personalities, 10 on Asma Ul Husna). It goes by the width of the
  index, not of the screen. A group the reader opens stays open when they come
  back from an entry, and a filter opens whatever it finds.
- **Four arrangements sit two by two** on a phone (400px and under): one row
  cannot hold "By Meaning, 1–99, A–Z, Most in Quran". A label there may wrap,
  for a reader whose browser doubles the text size.
- **A listing row stacks when it is very narrow** (under 17rem, which is a
  phone with the text size doubled): the Arabic or Urdu name goes under the
  name. The measure is the index's own width, in rem.
- **A theme's page keeps its surah index in reach.** Below a desktop's width it
  is one select stuck under the top of the screen, which always shows the
  surah being read, with a "top" link beside it. It replaced a swipeable row of
  up to 77 chips that was 12,000px wide and never showed where you were.
- **An ayah's number on a surah's page is a full tap target on touch**
  (2.75rem): it is the way to the ayah's tafsir and recitation.
- **An ayah's player gets its own row on a phone**, with Prev and Next under
  it at either end; three in one row left the player too narrow for its time.

An ayah's page keys off headings the generator writes (`## Arabic`,
`## English (…)`, `## Urdu (…)`, `## Themes`) and a surah's page off
`## Ayaat`, as the listings key off their classes. If those headings change,
the styling stops applying and the page degrades to plain prose. It doesn't
break.

Check any layout change at every one of these widths, and measure rather than
glance: nothing clipped, nothing overflowing its frame, nothing misaligned.

| Width          | Stands for                                                   |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 344px          | a foldable, closed (its narrow outer screen)                 |
| 390px          | a phone                                                      |
| 690px          | a foldable, open                                             |
| 768px          | a tablet, portrait: this one still gets the _mobile_ layout  |
| 820px          | a tablet, portrait: this one gets the tablet layout          |
| 1180px         | a tablet, landscape                                          |
| 1280px, 1512px | laptops                                                      |
| 1920px         | a desktop monitor                                            |
| 2560px         | a high-resolution display (the layout is capped and centred) |

The two foldable widths are roughly what a book-style foldable (a Galaxy Z Fold)
reports closed and open; treat them as stand-ins, not exact device figures. A
high-resolution display also means sharp pixels at any width, so check that
images and icons stay crisp at 2x and 3x.

## Graph View

The graph renders all 6,500+ nodes using D3 force simulation.
It is known to be slow on initial load. It is on every page but the front page
(`condition: not-index`) and the pages that take the right-hand column for
themselves (the listings, a theme's page, a surah's page, and since 2026-10-17
a person's and a Name's page).

If improving graph performance:

- Limit initial render depth to 1 (current node + direct connections)
- Cap maximum rendered nodes at ~200
- Reduce D3 simulation alphaDecay and velocityDecay
- Do NOT increase node count or disable depth limiting
- Test on mobile after any change — mobile is the primary bottleneck

`.base` files, if the generator writes any, are excluded from the graph via the graph patch (see above),
and the same patch skips the graph entirely on touch devices — so the
numbers above only ever apply to a desktop browser with a pointer.

## What NOT to Do

- Do not edit any file inside `/content/` — it is generated content
- Do not re-enable `og-image` — it breaks build times
- Do not enable a plugin that isn't in `package.json`, or one that needs a
  native package (e.g. `sharp`) without adding that too
- Do not use `^` ranges for patched plugins, or add Dependabot/Renovate
  bumps for them — a version change breaks the patch and the deploy
- Do not commit `node_modules/`
- Do not add audio files to this repo — use cdn.islamic.app
- Do not change `baseUrl` without also updating DNS and Cloudflare settings
- Do not edit `quartz.ts`
- Do not add a `prebuild` npm hook
- Do not use `100vh` for full-screen mobile UI — use `100dvh`
- Do not add chrome above the article on mobile without checking what it
  costs above the fold; the app bar and page header are already most of a
  phone screen before the first line of the verse
